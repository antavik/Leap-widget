# Accessibility

Leap Widget inherits the macOS accessibility settings you have already chosen. There
is nothing to turn on inside Leap Widget.

## VoiceOver

Each widget speaks as a single element, not as forty-two separate day
numbers. VoiceOver reads a short, useful summary:

- For the month that contains today: *"Today is Monday, 12 June 2026."*
- With **Week Numbers** on, the week is included: *"Today is Monday, 12 June
  2026, week 24."*

The date is formatted for your language and region.

To hear it, turn on VoiceOver (press Command-F5), then move the VoiceOver
cursor over the widget on the Desktop or in Notification Center.

Leap Widget's own window is just as direct: each of the three setup steps is a single
VoiceOver element, announced as *"Step 1 of 3"* with its full instruction,
rather than as an icon and two separate blocks of text.

## Increase Contrast

With **System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Display ▸ Increase contrast** turned
on, the superscript month number is drawn at full strength instead of the
dimmer default, so it stays legible against the wallpaper.

## Dark Mode and appearance

Leap Widget follows **System Settings ▸ Appearance** automatically, including Auto. It
uses the system's own widget materials, so its widgets are as readable as
Apple's over the same wallpaper.

## Reduce Motion

Leap Widget animates nothing. The widget changes only when the day does.

## Display size and zoom

Widget size is controlled by macOS, not by Leap Widget: a widget is small or medium
and keeps its proportions. To make it larger on screen, pick a scaled
resolution in **System Settings ▸ Displays**, or use **Accessibility ▸ Zoom**.

## Keyboard

Widgets are placed and edited through the macOS widget gallery, which is fully
keyboard navigable (Tab, arrow keys, Space). Inside Leap Widget's own window, standard
shortcuts apply - Command-W closes it, Command-Q quits, and Command-? opens
this manual.

## Privacy on screen

Leap Widget shows no event titles. Nothing private is ever drawn on your Desktop, so
its widgets are safe to leave on screen while you present or share your screen.
